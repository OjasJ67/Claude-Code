      The science

      Why the biology makes this work

      Both product lines rest on well-studied biology. Small numbers link to the sources below.

      Why one plant can become hundreds

      Many plant cells are totipotent: given the right conditions, a small piece of tissue can regenerate a whole new plant.15

      The balance of two plant hormones decides what grows. More cytokinin than auxin pushes tissue to make shoots; more auxin than cytokinin makes roots; a middle balance produces undifferentiated callus.2,3

      The Murashige & Skoog (MS) nutrient medium, published in 1962, is still the standard recipe for feeding plants in culture.1

      Carnivorous plants evolved in poor soils and multiply best on diluted medium: Venus flytraps at one-third strength4 and Drosera intermedia at one-quarter strength.5

      So what?

      Because every plantlet grows in a sterile jar from a clean parent, the plants arrive pest-free and identical, and one parent can supply hundreds of buyers with no wild collection.

      Why springtails and isopods clean a terrarium

      Isopods are crustaceans, relatives of shrimp and crabs, and the most successful crustaceans to colonize land.8

      Isopods break down plant litter mechanically and chemically and boost microbial activity. They prefer decaying leaves over fresh ones, because decaying litter is easier to digest.7

      Springtails are among the most abundant soil animals, at roughly 10,000–100,000 per square meter in most land ecosystems.11,12

      The springtail Folsomia candida feeds on fungal hyphae, reproduces without males, and is easy to culture on dry yeast. It has served as a standard soil test organism for more than 40 years.10

      So what?

      Because these animals naturally eat decay and mold and breed readily in captivity, a small starter culture becomes a self-sustaining cleanup crew.
